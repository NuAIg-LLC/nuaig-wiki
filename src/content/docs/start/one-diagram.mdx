## How to read it

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Two surfaces</dt><dd>The family portal and the staff workspace never share a trust level. The portal can only write into a holding area; staff promote what is in it.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>One system of record</dt><dd>Every rule about who may see what, what a figure means, and what may change lives here. A screen can hide a button, but it is never the thing that stops an action.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The calculation is separate from the advice</dt><dd>The deterministic calculation produces the recorded answer. The probability layer works from a frozen copy of that answer and returns commentary beside it.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The assistant proposes</dt><dd>Reading a document or drafting a plain-language summary is allowed. Setting a number, changing an outcome or recording a decision is structurally impossible for it.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The store enforces isolation</dt><dd>Separation between clients, and between communities, is a property of the database itself. A mistake in application logic cannot bypass it.</dd></div>
</div>

## Three questions this diagram answers

<ol class="steps not-content">
  <li>
    <h4>Where does a number come from?</h4>
    <p>From the calculation, using the policy and pricing that were in force, on inputs a named person verified. Never from the probability layer and never from the assistant.</p>
  </li>
  <li>
    <h4>Where is a rule enforced?</h4>
    <p>In the system of record. If a rule is only visible in a screen, it is not a rule.</p>
  </li>
  <li>
    <h4>Who is accountable for an outcome?</h4>
    <p>The person whose name is on the decision. The workflow shows exactly who recommended, who approved, and who signed.</p>
  </li>
</ol>
