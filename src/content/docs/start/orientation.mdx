This wiki is the product's written memory. It is meant to outlast any particular release,
any particular team, and any particular tool. It describes **what the product does and
why**, never how a given piece of it happens to be built this quarter.

## Three rules it follows

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Behaviour, not implementation</dt><dd>You will not find a programming language, framework or library named anywhere in these pages. Where a page needs to describe a mechanism, it describes the mechanism, not the tool that happens to provide it.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>No client identities</dt><dd>The platform serves several client organisations. Nothing here names one, and no example uses real resident data. Every figure and name in a screenshot is synthetic.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>One home per fact</dt><dd>Each fact is stated on exactly one page and linked from the others. If two pages disagree, the one nearer the top of the sidebar is the one to fix.</dd></div>
</div>

## The nine parts

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Start here</dt><dd>Orientation. What the product is, the whole system on one page, and the vocabulary.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Context</dt><dd>The business problem, the manual process being replaced, who pays and who uses it, and the numbered client requirements the product was built against.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>People and permissions</dt><dd>Tenancy, the eight roles, the capabilities each one holds, and the rules that decide what a given person may see.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Flows</dt><dd>The journey a case travels, broken into the nine processes that make it up. Read this before the screens.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The screens</dt><dd>Every screen in both applications, with a screenshot, the job it does, who can open it, and what it refuses to do.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>How the numbers work</dt><dd>The calculation models, their inputs and outputs, the outcome thresholds, the advisory probability layer, and the limits of all of it.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Interfaces</dt><dd>The programmatic surface: which resources exist, how changes are made safely, how failures are reported.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Architecture</dt><dd>The shape of the system, the records it keeps, and the controls around them.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Operations</dt><dd>Onboarding a client, governing configuration, retention, keys, and answering support questions.</dd></div>
</div>

## If you are new

Read, in order: **What this platform is**, **The problem being solved**, **The end-to-end
journey**, **User roles**, and **Two models, one product**. That is roughly forty minutes,
and it covers everything a conversation about the product will assume you already know.
Keep the **Glossary** open beside it.

## A note on what you will not find

This wiki deliberately does not record project status, sprint plans, delivery dates or
the state of any particular environment. Those change weekly and belong in the tools that
track them. Everything here is intended to be true for as long as the product behaves the
way it is described.
