This product serves several client organisations, and they differ. They differ in how many
approvals a decision needs, in what they call a level of care, in whether a refund counts
towards a threshold. The rule that handles every one of those differences is the same, and
it is strict:

> **No client-specific behaviour is ever written into the product. A difference between
> clients is a setting whose default preserves current behaviour, and it is documented.**

## Why the rule has teeth

A product that quietly becomes several products cannot be reasoned about. Every special
case is a permanent cost, a place where one client's behaviour can surprise another, and a
fact someone has to remember. The rule keeps the product one product.

## What is configured, and by whom

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Life-plan policy (organisation)</dt><dd>Approved thresholds or none, the contract catalogue with allowed refund rates, named care duration profiles, annual rates for investment return, income increase, expense increase and a separate fee increase for each level of care, the insurance weight, the maximum projection length, and the five conventions. Published by a client administrator.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Life-plan pricing (community)</dt><dd>Which central contracts the community offers with an entrance fee and refund rate for each, a monthly fee for each level of care, and the second-person fee. A community can never change central durations, thresholds or rates.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Unit schedules</dt><dd>Dated per-unit prices with a source, plus the list of named price-reduction approvers.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Qualification review policy</dt><dd>Independent prerequisites and who may complete them, committee slots, how long a review stays valid, and the named waiver and escalation approvers. Set for the organisation and optionally overridden for a community.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Assessment policy</dt><dd>The runway model's tier cutoffs, look-back, state limits and pricing, centrally, with monthly care prices overridable per facility.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Family portal</dt><dd>Name, accent colour, welcome text, contact person, and which sections and document types are requested.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Communications</dt><dd>Whether outbound messages are enabled and the gateways used.</dd></div>
</div>

## The five conventions of the life-plan model

These are the clearest examples of the rule. Each default preserves the model's original
behaviour.

| Setting | Options | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Return convention | Monthly on the declining balance, or annual at opening | Monthly on the declining balance |
| Refund counts towards threshold | Yes or no | Yes |
| Couple leaves independent living | Independently, or both on the earlier date | Independently |
| Duration source | Named profiles, or actuarial tables (not loaded, so refused) | Named profiles |
| Care level labels | Any words, for the four fixed levels | Independent living, assisted living, memory care, skilled nursing |

## Publishing a change

A policy is never edited in place. It is **published as a new version**, and publishing
follows the same rules as every other change in the product:

<ol class="steps not-content">
  <li><h4>Read the current configuration</h4><p>The read returns an opaque revision token describing exactly what the editor is looking at.</p></li>
  <li><h4>Publish with the expected revision</h4><p>The token is sent back with the change and an idempotency key. The store takes a lock for the organisation and compares.</p></li>
  <li><h4>A mismatch is a conflict</h4><p>If someone else published first, the change is refused with a conflict. The administrator reloads and decides. The product never rebases for them.</p></li>
  <li><h4>A match creates a new version</h4><p>The earlier version remains. Nothing is overwritten.</p></li>
</ol>

Publishing a central change also checks the community assignments that depend on it, so a
catalogue edit cannot silently invalidate a community's pricing.

## Unset means unset

The product ships **no approved financial assumptions**: no thresholds, no growth rates, no
care durations, no prices. A fresh organisation has an empty policy, and the product says so
instead of filling the gap. Until the client publishes approved thresholds, the outcome of a
projection is *unconfigured*, and a qualification cannot be completed.

This is a feature. A default threshold that a client forgot to review would quietly become
their policy. An empty one cannot.
