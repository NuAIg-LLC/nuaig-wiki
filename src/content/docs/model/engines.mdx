The product contains two calculation models. They answer different questions, they are
kept strictly apart, and confusing them is the single most common way to misunderstand the
numbers. This page says which is which.

## Side by side

| | Runway model | Life-plan model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Question** | How long can they pay the monthly bill? | Does their money outlast the whole residency? |
| **People** | One | One or two, with survivor effects |
| **Horizon** | The planned length of stay | As long as the longer-lived resident |
| **Cost** | One flat price for the level of care | Fees that rise yearly, differently at each level of care |
| **Care levels** | A single level, with an informational escalation | A sequence through four levels, per resident |
| **Entrance fee** | None | Taken at month zero, with a contingent refund |
| **Investment** | Not modelled | Earned on positive balances |
| **Result** | Runway, funding gap, Medicaid crossover, tier | Month-by-month balance, shortfall, estate value, outcome |
| **Classification** | Four risk tiers | Three outcomes plus an unconfigured state |
| **Policy** | Tier cutoffs, state limits, look-back | Thresholds, rates, durations, contracts, conventions |
| **Stored as** | An assessment | A qualification |

## Why there are two

The runway model came first and answers a real question for a community whose residents pay
a monthly fee and nothing else. A community that sells a **lifetime contract** has a
different problem: a large entrance fee, a contract with a contingent refund, and decades
of changing care. Patching one model to serve both would have changed the meaning of results
already on record, so they are separate calculations with separate configuration and
separate stored records.

**For a community that sells entrance-fee contracts, the life-plan model is the instrument.**
The runway model is retained for monthly-fee communities and for the quick scenarios in the
<a href="/screens/modeling/">projection lab</a>.

## What neither model ever uses

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>Characteristics of the person</h4>
    <p>No age, date of birth, sex, name, identity number or diagnosis. The input is a strict shape that rejects any such field outright.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>The clock</h4>
    <p>No calculation reads today's date. Every date is an explicit input.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>A language model</h4>
    <p>The assistant may describe a result in words after the fact. It never takes part in producing one.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>Approved defaults</h4>
    <p>The life-plan model ships with no thresholds, rates or durations. The client supplies and approves them.</p>
  </div>
</div>

Why this matters: choosing a projected length of stay from someone's age and sex is a
decision about how a protected characteristic may be used to price a service. That decision
belongs to the client and their counsel, not to a calculation.

## The third layer: advice

A recorded qualification can be examined further by **advanced analysis**, a seeded
probability simulation that reports how a household behaves across many plausible futures.
It reads a frozen copy of the qualification and returns commentary stored beside it. It
cannot set or change an outcome. See <a href="/model/simulation/">Advanced analysis</a>.
