The result of a life-plan projection is a set of figures. The **outcome** is the product's
classification of those figures against thresholds the client has approved. It exists so
that "marginal" means the same thing for every community in an organisation.

## The four states

| Outcome | When it applies |
| --- | --- |
| **Unconfigured** | The client has not published approved thresholds. The projection is valid and readable but cannot be completed as a qualification. |
| **Not qualified** | Any month's balance went negative, **or** the threshold basis is below the marginal floor. |
| **Consider another unit** | Every month funded, and the basis is at or above the marginal floor but below the definite-fit floor. |
| **Definite fit** | Every month funded, and the basis is at or above the definite-fit floor. |

## The thresholds

Two numbers, set centrally by the client's finance leadership and published with a version:

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>The marginal floor</dt><dd>The lowest basis at which a household is worth considering. Below it, not qualified.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The definite-fit floor</dt><dd>The basis at which a household is a definite fit. It must be strictly above the marginal floor, and the product refuses a policy where it is not.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The approval flag</dt><dd>Thresholds carry an explicit approved marker. Publishing without it records no thresholds, and the outcome stays unconfigured.</dd></div>
</div>

Either floor may be negative or zero, since they are amounts in whole cents. The product
supplies no value for either. A figure mentioned in a meeting is an example and never a
default.

## What the thresholds are measured against

The **basis** is a client setting:

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel panel--info">
    <h4>Estate value (default)</h4>
    <p>The ending balance plus the contingent refund. This follows how many operators already think about the refundable entrance fee.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--info">
    <h4>Ending balance only</h4>
    <p>The refund is excluded. A client may prefer this because the refund is estate value, not liquidity, and counting it can overstate the cushion in marginal cases.</p>
  </div>
</div>

The reported estate balance is the same either way. Only the measuring stick changes. The
<a href="/model/worked-example/">worked example</a> shows one household switching outcome
on this setting alone.

## The cash-flow check

The check that matters most is not a threshold at all. If **any month closes with a
negative balance**, the outcome is not qualified, however large the estate is.

This is deliberate. A household that cannot pay for care in one month has a real problem
in that month, and it does not go away because a house sells later or a refund is due
decades on. The projection keeps the first such month on the record, and the screen says
so in words.

## What an outcome is not

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>An admission decision</h4>
    <p>The outcome is financial. A person, through the approval chain, decides whether to admit. See <a href="/flows/decision/">Review, sign-off and decision</a>.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>A prediction</h4>
    <p>It classifies a single projection under stated assumptions. It says nothing about how likely the assumptions are. See <a href="/model/simulation/">Advanced analysis</a>.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>Permanent</h4>
    <p>A qualification pins the policy and pricing it used. A change in thresholds later requires a new qualification. It never changes an old one.</p>
  </div>
</div>
