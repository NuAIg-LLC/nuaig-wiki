Numbers make the model easier to trust than prose does. This household is deliberately
tiny, a four-month residency, so every line can be checked by hand. The figures were
produced by running the real calculation, and each is reproduced below.

## Month by month

**Month zero.** $320,000 of savings, minus the $200,000 entrance fee, leaves **$120,000**.

| Month | Care level | Income | Insurance recognised | Care fee | Other | Closing balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Independent living | $3,500 | $0 | $4,000 | $800 | **$118,700** |
| 2 | Independent living | $3,500 | $0 | $4,000 | $800 | **$117,400** |
| 3 | Assisted living | $3,500 | $1,500 | $6,500 | $800 | **$115,100** |
| 4 | Skilled nursing | $3,500 | $1,500 | $9,000 | $800 | **$110,300** |

Two things are worth noticing. **Insurance is recognised at $1,500, not $3,000**, because
the central weight is 50 percent. And it only appears in months three and four, because
those are the only months at the covered levels of care.

## Variation one: leave the refund out

The refund only pays out when both residents have left and the unit has been resold. Some
clients choose not to count it towards the threshold.

With that one setting changed, the thresholds are measured against the ending balance of
**$110,300** instead of the estate value. That is above the $50,000 marginal floor and below
the $150,000 definite-fit floor, so the outcome becomes **consider another unit**. The
projected figures are identical. Only the classification differs.

## Variation two: add growth

Now assume 4.5 percent investment return, fees rising 5 percent, 6 percent, 6.5 percent and
6.5 percent a year for the four levels, adjustable income rising 2 percent, and expenses
rising 3 percent. Returns are earned each month on the positive balance.

| Month | Return earned | Care fee | Closing balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $440.98 | $4,000.00 | $119,140.98 |
| 2 | $437.82 | $4,016.30 | $118,263.01 |
| 3 | $434.59 | $6,563.43 | $116,335.18 |
| 4 | $427.51 | $9,142.81 | $111,821.39 |

Total investment return across the four months is **$1,740.90**, and the ending balance
rises to **$111,821.39**. Notice that the first month's fee is unchanged, because the
first planning month always uses current prices, and that each level's fee has grown by
its own rate by the time it applies.

## Variation three: a shortfall that a later sale cannot erase

Change the household so it starts with $203,000 of savings, and add a house whose net
proceeds of $300,000 arrive in month four.

| Month | Closing balance |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | $3,000 |
| 1 | $1,700 |
| 2 | $400 |
| 3 | **-$1,900** |
| 4 | $293,300 |

In month three the household cannot pay. The balance is negative: **a funding shortfall**.
When the house sale lands in month four the balance recovers, and the estate value,
including the refund, is **$393,300**, far above every threshold.

The outcome is nevertheless **not qualified**. A positive estate cannot hide a month in
which care could not be paid for. The model keeps the first shortfall month on the record,
and its warning says that later proceeds or a refund do not erase it.

## Variation four: no approved thresholds

Run the original household against a policy where the client has not yet approved
thresholds. Every figure above is calculated, but the outcome is **unconfigured**. The
projection can be read and discussed. It cannot be completed as a qualification until the
client publishes approved thresholds.

## What to take from it

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>The calculation is simple, and that is the point</dt><dd>Every figure is a sum a person can check. There is no hidden step.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Conventions matter more than arithmetic</dt><dd>Whether the refund counts, and whether returns are credited monthly or annually, change the classification of a marginal household. That is why they are settings the client chooses and documents.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>A shortfall is a fact about a month</dt><dd>Not about the ending balance. That is why a refund or a later sale can never rescue it.</dd></div>
</div>
