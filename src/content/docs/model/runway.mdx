The runway model is a simple, fully explainable question: **given what this person has and
what they earn, how long can they pay, and what is missing if they stay as long as
planned?** Read <a href="/model/engines/">Two models, one product</a> first if you are
unsure when it applies.

## What goes in

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Monthly income</dt><dd>What the resident receives each month, plus any recurring contribution from a responsible party.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Liquid assets</dt><dd>Cash and holdings that can be drawn on.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Home equity</dt><dd>With a flag for whether it counts towards the Medicaid asset test.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Long-term care benefit</dt><dd>A monthly amount and how many months remain, or that it continues without limit.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Recent transfers</dt><dd>The amount moved recently and how long ago the largest one was.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Care level and state</dt><dd>The level of care, which sets the monthly cost, and the state, which sets the Medicaid limits.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Planned length of stay</dt><dd>The horizon, in months, that the funding gap is measured over.</dd></div>
</div>

Nothing about who the person is enters the calculation. Care level is allowed, because it
is a legitimate, non-protected factor that drives cost.

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Net monthly burn</dt><dd>The resolved monthly care cost, minus income and contribution, minus any long-term care benefit that applies. Positive means money is leaving.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Covers cost</dt><dd>When net burn is zero or negative, income already covers the bill. Then there is no runway to report and no Medicaid crossover.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Private-pay runway</dt><dd>Run the months forward, paying each month's shortfall from liquid assets. Runway is the first month the assets cannot cover it.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Funding gap</dt><dd>Add up every month's shortfall across the planned stay, then subtract liquid assets. Never less than zero.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Medicaid crossover</dt><dd>Run the same months against countable assets, which are liquid assets plus home equity if it counts. Crossover is the month they fall to the state's asset limit.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Look-back</dt><dd>A transfer above a small de minimis amount, made inside the look-back window, is a violation. The estimated penalty period is the transfer divided by a state private-pay figure.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Care escalation</dt><dd>How the level of care, and so the cost, could step up over the stay. Informational: it informs the story and does not move the tier.</dd></div>
</div>

## How the tier is set

The tier starts at the level the runway implies and is then raised, never lowered, by
three further rules. Every rule that applies is recorded in the result.

| Rule | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Income covers cost | Lowest tier. No runway is reported. |
| Runway below the high cutoff | Highest tier |
| Runway below the elevated cutoff | Elevated |
| Runway below the moderate cutoff | Moderate |
| Runway at or above the moderate cutoff | Lowest tier |
| A look-back violation | At least elevated |
| Medicaid crossover within the imminent window, and income does not cover cost | At least elevated |
| A funding gap exists, and income does not cover cost | At least moderate |

The cutoffs, the imminent window, the look-back window, the de minimis amount, the state
asset limits and the penalty divisors are all **policy**, not code. They are published and
versioned by the client, so compliance can read and approve the numbers without reading any
program.

## Health flags

A resident may carry health flags in five categories: cognitive, mobility, clinical,
behavioural and social. A flag can shorten the interval at which the projected level of care
steps up. It appears as a factor in the explanation. It **never** changes the tier, and it
is removed from view entirely for anyone without the health scope.

## Explainability

Alongside the tier the result carries the **rules that fired** and a list of **factors**:
labelled, severity-graded, each with a sentence of evidence. A minimised copy of the
figures, with no names or account numbers, is what the assistant uses to draft the plain
summary. See <a href="/model/ai-boundary/">Where AI starts and stops</a>.
