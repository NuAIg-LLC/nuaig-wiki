Leadership needs a view across communities, and the product refuses to give it by the
obvious route. A tenant-wide "see everything" capability would turn one administrator
mistake into an organisation-wide disclosure. So reporting is built from permissions that
are **explicit, narrow and individually revocable**.

## Granting access

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Eligibility</dt><dd>The person must be an active user, in an active community, with an active membership holding the required read scopes. Platform administrators and emergency identities can never be granted routine reporting.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Versioned</dt><dd>Each grant carries a version. A stale write conflicts instead of overwriting, and a grant that exists outside the page being edited causes a conflict rather than being silently duplicated.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Revocable at any time</dt><dd>Including after the person's membership has been removed.</dd></div>
</div>

## Running a report

<Shot
  src="/img/shots/reports-run.webp"
  route="/reports/life-plan"
  role="Finance reviewer"
  caption="<b>Qualification reports.</b> Choose up to twenty granted communities and a date range, then run. Each row shows the original outcome, any funding shortfall, the ending balance, the separate contingent refund and the latest observed outcome."
/>

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Choices</dt><dd>Only communities the person is currently allowed to report on are offered. They select up to twenty, and the period is at most 366 days. Dates default to the last thirty days and include the full selected days in UTC.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Every request is re-checked</dt><dd>The user, organisation, community, membership role and grant are verified each time, and the rows that establish them are locked while the community is read, so access cannot be revoked mid-report without the report noticing.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>One community at a time</dt><dd>Each community is read inside its own isolation boundary and the results are merged. There is no tenant-wide bypass of the resident tables.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>All or nothing</dt><dd>If any one selected community is not authorised, the whole request is rejected rather than quietly omitting it.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Stable paging</dt><dd>Results sort by original completion time, newest first, with a sealed cursor bound to the person, the organisation, the selected communities and the dates, so a page cannot be replayed under different filters.</dd></div>
</div>

## What a row contains

A row is a **minimum-necessary history**: the resident and community names, the policy and
pricing the qualification used, when it was completed, its original outcome and financial
summary, and the most recent observation. It deliberately excludes the original
instrument lines, the full monthly projection and every private note.

## Two honest caveats the screen states

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>Counts describe the page</h4>
    <p>The summary figures at the top count the rows shown, not the whole query. The screen labels them that way, so nobody mistakes a page for a population.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>A forecast is not an outcome</h4>
    <p>A qualification is a forecast, not an admission decision and not an observed result. Reports keep the three apart.</p>
  </div>
</div>

A report row links to the resident's summary only for the community currently selected.
Opening a resident in another community still requires selecting that community through the
ordinary membership flow.
