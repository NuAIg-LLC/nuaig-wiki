## Starting an application

A counselor creates an application for a named primary contact and chooses how to reach
them, by email or by text message. The product does three things in one step:

- Creates the application, optionally attached to an existing resident record
- Issues a secure invitation bound to that contact and that channel
- Queues the message that carries the invitation link

The message itself is sent by a separate delivery process, after the change is safely
recorded. See <a href="/interfaces/notifications/">Notifications</a> for why.

## Letting the family in

The link alone is deliberately not enough. It is easy to forward, so it only identifies the
application. Proof of who is holding it is a one-time code.

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>The code</dt><dd>Six digits, stored only as a hash, valid for ten minutes by default, with a cooldown between requests and a lock after five wrong attempts. Each of these is a deployment setting.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The invitation</dt><dd>Valid for fourteen days by default. It can be revoked at any time, and an expired invitation has its code cleared.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The session</dt><dd>Short, with an absolute maximum lifetime. It is scoped to the single application the invitation was for, and it is created fresh each time a live link is opened, so a device left open cannot carry over to the next family.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Enumeration safety</dt><dd>Asking for a code gives the same answer whether or not the invitation is valid, so the form cannot be used to discover which links exist.</dd></div>
</div>

## What the family does

The guided application is made of up to four sections, each of which the client can switch
on or off, plus a document step.

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>About your loved one</dt><dd>Name, date of birth, state, and the level of care they need. A national identity number is optional and only requested if the family has it to hand.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Financial picture</dt><dd>Monthly income, liquid assets, and any long-term care benefit. Entered in everyday dollars and converted to whole cents only when staff promote the application.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Care needs</dt><dd>The diagnosis, the planned length of stay, and any health flags.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Who is helping</dt><dd>The responsible party, their relationship, any monthly contribution, and their explicit consent.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Documents</dt><dd>Uploaded in bulk and tagged by the family with a document type. Nothing is read or interpreted until staff promote.</dd></div>
</div>

Progress saves section by section, so a family can stop and return. Everything a family
types is treated as **staged and unverified**. It never reaches a calculation until a
person has confirmed it.

## Helpers get a narrow grant, never the whole application

Families rely on other people: a physician to describe care needs, a power of attorney who
signs, an adviser who holds the statements. Each of them has a narrow, legitimate reason to
touch one part of an application, and no reason to see the rest.

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel panel--ok">
    <h4>A helper grant is an allowlist</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Which sections they may see and edit</li>
      <li>Which document types they may upload</li>
      <li>Whether they may submit</li>
      <li>A note explaining what is being asked</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>Everything else is refused</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>A grant that permits nothing is rejected as a dead invite</li>
      <li>Anything added to the application later is invisible to existing helpers until the owner grants it</li>
      <li>Only the owner always holds the whole application</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

The kinds of helper offered are family, clinician, power of attorney, financial adviser and
other. Each starts from the narrowest preset that kind of person plausibly needs.
