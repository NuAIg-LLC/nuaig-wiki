The **assessment** is the monthly-fee view of a resident's finances. It answers the
question a community without a large entrance fee asks: how many months can this person pay
the bill, and what is missing if they stay as long as planned? For communities that sell
lifetime contracts, the <a href="/flows/qualification/">life-plan qualification</a> is the
primary instrument. Both models are described in <a href="/model/engines/">Two models, one product</a>.

## What the calculation does

It models one person, one flat monthly price for the level of care, and the household's
monthly income against it. From that it produces:

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Private-pay runway</dt><dd>The number of months liquid assets can cover the monthly shortfall. If income already covers the cost there is no runway to report.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Net monthly burn</dt><dd>Cost less income, family contribution and any long-term care benefit. Positive means money is going out.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Funding gap</dt><dd>The total shortfall over the planned length of stay, less what liquid assets can cover. Zero when the money lasts.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Medicaid crossover</dt><dd>The month in which countable assets reach the state's asset limit, with the look-back for recent transfers and an estimated penalty period.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Care escalation</dt><dd>How the projected care level, and therefore the cost, could rise over the stay. Informational: it informs the narrative and never moves the tier.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Risk tier and fired rules</dt><dd>One of four tiers, together with the exact rules that produced it. See <a href="/model/runway/">The runway model</a>.</dd></div>
</div>

## Explainability is the point

Every result lists **named factors**, each with a short sentence of evidence, and the
**rules that fired**. Someone reading the result can see not just that a resident is
high risk, but that the runway fell below the high-risk cutoff, that a recent transfer
fell inside the look-back window, and which figures made it so.

Health flags can annotate the care trajectory and the narrative. They **never** feed the
tier. This keeps the tier a function of money alone.

## Overriding a result

A finance reviewer, and only a finance reviewer, can mark a result as overridden.

## Keeping a decision honest

The executive director's decision must cite the **latest** assessment. Before it is
recorded, the stored result is compared with a fresh calculation from the current verified
data and the policy in force. If a figure, the policy or the calculation version has
changed since, the decision is refused and a new assessment is required. A stale result can
never be signed.

## What a counselor sees

The same result is served to every role that may read assessments, with dollar figures
removed for anyone without the financial scope. A counselor sees the tier, the runway in
months and the rules, but not the amounts. See
<a href="/people/minimum-necessary/">Minimum necessary access</a>.
