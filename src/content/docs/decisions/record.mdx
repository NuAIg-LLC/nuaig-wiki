A product that must be maintained for decades needs its reasons written down, because the
people who made a choice are not the people who will be tempted to undo it. Each decision
below is stated the same way: **what was decided, why, and what it costs.**

## Calculation and the assistant

### 1. Deterministic arithmetic, probabilistic language

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>Only versioned, pure, deterministic code computes anything that decides. A language model may extract fields from documents and draft plain-language summaries, and nothing else.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>A decision about whether someone can live somewhere must be explainable, reproducible and free of protected-class inputs. Language models are none of those, and are exactly what is wanted for reading and writing.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Extraction always needs a person to verify it, which is slower than trusting it. That is the price of the boundary.</dd></div>
</div>

### 2. Money in whole cents, and no clock

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>Every amount is an integer count of cents, every rate an integer count of basis points, and no calculation reads the current date.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>A recorded figure is evidence and must reproduce exactly years later. Decimal arithmetic loses cents invisibly, and a formula that reads today's date gives a different answer when reopened.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Every date a calculation needs must be an explicit input, and rounding rules must be chosen and documented rather than inherited.</dd></div>
</div>

### 3. A separate life-plan model, not a patched runway model

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>The lifetime model is its own calculation with its own configuration and its own stored record, beside the older runway model.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>The two answer different questions. Stretching one to serve both would have changed the meaning of results already on record.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Two models to understand and maintain, and a rule that new work attaches to the lifetime model. See <a href="/model/engines/">Two models, one product</a>.</dd></div>
</div>

### 4. No demographic or health input to duration

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>Care durations come from named profiles a reviewer chooses. There is no age, sex or health input, and the model refuses an actuarial source rather than silently substituting.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>Choosing a stay from a protected characteristic is a legal decision about how a service may be priced. It belongs to the client's counsel, as a configured policy, with its own review.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Less automation than a spreadsheet that does this implicitly, and an explicit choice at the table every time.</dd></div>
</div>

### 5. The refund is estate value, never cash, and a shortfall cannot be outweighed

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>The contingent refund is reported separately and never funds care. Any month with a negative balance makes the outcome not qualified, whatever the estate. A client setting decides whether the refund counts towards thresholds.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>The refund depends on both residents leaving and the unit being resold. Counting it as cash overstates the cushion exactly where it decides the answer.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Some households that look wealthy on paper are not qualified, and that needs explaining at the table.</dd></div>
</div>

### 6. Probability advises, structurally

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>Advanced analysis runs in a service with no database credential, stores into a shape with no outcome field, in a table that accepts inserts only. It is seeded and reproducible, and verified against the model to the cent.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>An advisory number that can leak into a decision is a decision made by something nobody can explain.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>A second implementation of the monthly model to keep in step, managed by a parity check that fails if they diverge.</dd></div>
</div>

## Isolation and security

### 7. Isolation is enforced by the store

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>One shared store with row-level isolation, set per transaction on two axes, organisation and community. The application connects as an identity that cannot bypass it. A physically separate store is a higher tier, not the baseline.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>A rule enforced only in application code is broken by one forgotten filter, invisibly. A rule enforced by the store fails closed.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Every transaction must establish its context, and a small number of legitimate cross-boundary operations need hardened, individually reviewed lookups.</dd></div>
</div>

### 8. The family portal is a separate application with its own identity

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>The portal can reach only a staging area, through its own database identity and a per-application isolation axis. Staff promote what is staged into the record.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>It is the highest-risk surface. Bolting a family session onto the staff identity with an extra filter would make one missed clause a breach.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>A promotion step, and two codebases to keep consistent.</dd></div>
</div>

### 9. Helpers get an allowlist, never the whole application

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>A person a family invites holds an explicit grant of sections and document types. Anything not granted is refused, and a grant that permits nothing is rejected.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>Families rely on physicians, advisers and attorneys who each need one part of a file. "Do it alone or hand over everything" pushes people to the worse choice.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>More to configure for the owner, and more states to reason about.</dd></div>
</div>

### 10. Identity numbers are tokenised and held apart, and fields are encrypted by purpose

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>The full identity number lives in a separate store and is never returned to anyone. Sensitive fields are stored as ciphertext under purpose-specific keys.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>One record concentrates identity, health and financial data. Shrinking what a leak yields, and what any one key unlocks, limits the damage.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Encrypted columns cannot be queried directly, so what must be searched is kept as separate, deliberate metadata.</dd></div>
</div>

### 11. Minimum necessary, applied when the response is built

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>A response contains only what the caller's role may see, and says when something was withheld.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>A field sent to a browser and hidden has been disclosed.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Every screen must handle absent data gracefully, and explain who owns a blocked step.</dd></div>
</div>

### 12. A hash-chained, per-organisation audit ledger

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>Every read and write of regulated data appends an event containing the hash of the previous one, one chain per organisation, written in the same transaction as the change.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>The trail is a product feature, a legal need and a financial control, and must resist a malicious writer, not only a careless one.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Appends are serialised per organisation, and the chain is tamper-evident rather than tamper-proof.</dd></div>
</div>

## Authority and accountability

### 13. Two independent authorities: administration and resident access

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>Administering an organisation never grants access to its residents, and administering a building does not require joining it. Configuration names its target explicitly.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>The person who sets the product up is usually not the person who should read a family's finances, and an editor that silently followed the selected building made two administrators change different things from one link.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>A person who needs both has to hold both deliberately.</dd></div>
</div>

### 14. The platform operator carries no resident-data capability

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>The maker's role provisions, activates, suspends and repairs organisations through a separate surface and a separate namespace, and can read no resident data. Every action lands in the target organisation's own ledger.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>Running a platform must not turn the operator into a member of every client's clinical and financial workforce.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Some support tasks need the client's own administrator, since the maker cannot look.</dd></div>
</div>

### 15. Approvers are named, independent and never inferred

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>Waivers, escalations and price reductions are answered by explicitly named people, never the requester, who must currently hold the right role. An empty list grants nothing. Committee slots need distinct people, separate from the recommender and the signer.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>An approval that anyone with a similar job title can give is not an approval.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>A client must name people up front, and keep the lists current.</dd></div>
</div>

### 16. A conflict is an answer; a retry is never a second change

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>Edits name the revision they were based on and conflict if the record moved. Changes carry an idempotency key. The platform never rebases and retries on its own.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>Silent retry applies a decision to facts the person never saw, and a repeated request must not duplicate.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>People see more conflicts than they would with silent merging, and clients must handle them.</dd></div>
</div>

### 17. Evidence is immutable and pins its inputs

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>Qualifications, reviews, decisions, observations, analyses and price records are insert-only by permission. A qualification pins the policy and pricing it used. A correction is a new record.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>These records may be evidence years later. If history can be rewritten, it is not history.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Storage grows, and a mistake is corrected by adding, not removing.</dd></div>
</div>

## Operation

### 18. Messages are queued with the change and delivered afterwards

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>A message is written in the same transaction as the change, and a separate worker delivers it, with signed callbacks and an explicit terminal state.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>Calling a provider inside the transaction produces messages for things that never happened, or things with no message, and neither can be repaired.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>A separate worker to run and monitor, and delivery that is eventual, not instant.</dd></div>
</div>

### 19. No client-specific behaviour, and no approved defaults

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>Differences between clients are settings whose defaults preserve current behaviour. The product ships no thresholds, rates, durations or prices.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>Special cases accumulate into several products, and a default nobody reviewed quietly becomes a client's policy.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>A new organisation has to supply its numbers before it can complete a qualification, which feels slower at the start.</dd></div>
</div>

### 20. Protection at the edge is separate from where regulated data lives

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>Filtering and access control in front of the platform are an edge concern that persists nothing. Regulated data is processed and stored only on infrastructure covered by the agreements that must cover it.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>An agreement that covers the edge does not necessarily cover persisting regulated data there, and the question "where does it live" should be short.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Two arrangements to manage instead of one.</dd></div>
</div>

### 21. Port behaviour exactly, improve it separately

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Decided</dt><dd>When behaviour is reproduced from an earlier implementation, it is copied exactly. An improvement is its own change with its own test and recorded reason.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Because</dt><dd>An unrecorded improvement is indistinguishable from a regression, and some behaviours that look odd are deliberate guards.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>It costs</dt><dd>Known imperfections survive until they are deliberately fixed.</dd></div>
</div>
