Occasionally a person genuinely needs to read something their role does not allow. The
usual example is an urgent situation where the right person is unavailable. The product
supports this through **emergency access**, commonly called break-glass, and it is built
so that using it is deliberate, bounded and loud.

## What it grants, and what it does not

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel panel--ok">
    <h4>It does</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Add the read half of every scope for the duration of the grant</li>
      <li>Let a person see across the minimum-necessary boundary</li>
      <li>Record every access as an alert-level audit event</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>It does not</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Grant any write, override, record or manage authority</li>
      <li>Reach another client organisation, ever</li>
      <li>Survive its expiry or a revocation</li>
      <li>Work without a written justification</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

## Rules worth knowing

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>A role is not a grant</dt><dd>Holding the break-glass role confers nothing on its own. Read access is added only while a specific, unrevoked, unexpired grant is presented.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Duration is bounded</dt><dd>A grant lasts a minimum of 5 and a maximum of 60 minutes. If more time is genuinely needed, a new grant is issued, which is a new decision and a new record.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Justification is mandatory</dt><dd>Both when issuing a grant and when each protected read happens. A request without one is refused, not logged and allowed.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Isolation is absolute</dt><dd>The organisation boundary is checked before anything else. Emergency access widens what a person may see inside their own organisation, never whose data they may see.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Revocation is audited</dt><dd>Ending a grant early records who did it and why, and that record cannot be edited afterwards.</dd></div>
</div>
