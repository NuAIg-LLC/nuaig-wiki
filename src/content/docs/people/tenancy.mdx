## The three ideas

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Client organisation</dt><dd>The customer. The hard boundary of the product: nothing in one organisation is ever visible from another, and no role, including the platform operator, can reach across it to read residents. The boundary is enforced by the data store itself.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Community</dt><dd>A single building within an organisation. Residents, applications, qualifications and unit pricing belong to a community. A second, independent boundary sits here: a person can be inside the organisation and still not see a particular building's residents.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Membership</dt><dd>A person's link to one community, with a role in it. A person can hold different roles in different communities, so the same person may be a finance reviewer in one building and a counselor in another. Membership is what grants access to that building's residents.</dd></div>
</div>

## The rule that surprises people

**Administering a community does not require belonging to it, and does not grant access to its residents.**

A client administrator manages staff, buildings, policy, portal branding and notification
settings across the whole organisation. They name the building they want to configure in
the address of the page they are editing, and they never have to join it. Nothing they do
gives them a view of any resident.

This is deliberate. The person who sets up the product for an organisation is usually not
the person who should read a family's finances.

## What is shared, and what is local

Settings resolve in layers, each narrowing the one above. A layer may only change what the
layer above has explicitly opened to it.

## Why isolation is checked twice

Isolation has two independent axes, and every resident record is checked against both:

1. **The organisation boundary.** A request carries the organisation it belongs to. Records in any other organisation are invisible, not forbidden. Asking for one returns "not found", which reveals nothing, not even that it exists.
2. **The community boundary.** For resident data the request also carries the community it is acting in. Staff may only act in a community they hold a membership for, and records in other communities are invisible in the same way.

Both axes **fail closed**: if the request carries no organisation, or no community, the
store returns no rows at all rather than all of them. See
<a href="/architecture/isolation/">Isolation and tenancy</a> for how this is enforced.
