## What is cleaned up

A maintenance task runs on a schedule and removes data that has no evidentiary value:

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Family sessions</dt><dd>Expired or revoked sessions.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Idempotency records</dt><dd>Stored answers older than the operational retention period, never one that is still in progress.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Rate-limit counters</dt><dd>Counters that have reset.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Expired invitations</dt><dd>Revoked, and their one-time codes cleared, even though the invitation record itself is kept.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Delivery records</dt><dd>Delivered or failed messages older than a period the client chooses. If no period is set, none are removed.</dd></div>
</div>

The periods are deployment settings. The task reports what it removed and is safe to run
repeatedly.

## What is never automatic

Resident records, documents, qualifications, decisions, observations, policy versions and
the audit ledger carry **no automatic deletion**, and several are immutable by permission.
This is deliberate. Deleting evidence on a timer, on a guessed schedule, is a harder mistake
to undo than keeping it too long.

## What belongs to the client

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Retention periods by class</dt><dd>How long each class of record must be kept: identity, health, financial, derived output and documents. These differ by jurisdiction and by contract, and they are the client's legal advisers' to set.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Legal holds</dt><dd>Suspending disposal because of a dispute or an inquiry.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Disposal and export</dt><dd>How a record is reviewed, approved for disposal, destroyed and proved destroyed, and what must be exported first.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Individual requests</dt><dd>How a resident's or a family's request to see or delete their data is received, verified and answered.</dd></div>
</div>

Until the client has approved a retention schedule, the safe position is the product's
default: **keep everything evidentiary, and tidy only the plumbing**.

## What the product provides to help

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>A class on every field</h4>
    <p>Each sensitive field is classified on intake, so a schedule can be written and applied by class instead of record by record.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">

  <div class="panel">

  </div>

## Responding to a deletion or access request

Because disposal is not automated, a request is handled as a **procedure** with a named
owner:

<ol class="steps not-content">
  <li><h4>Receive and verify the request</h4><p>And who is asking, and on whose behalf.</p></li>
  <li><h4>Check for holds and obligations to retain</h4><p>A legal duty to keep a record can outweigh a request to delete it.</p></li>
  <li><h4>Decide, and record the decision</h4><p>By the client's authority, with the reason.</p></li>
  <li><h4>Carry it out through a controlled administrative change</h4><p>Not an ad hoc edit. Every step should be reviewable.</p></li>
  <li><h4>Confirm and keep the evidence</h4><p>That the request was handled, and what was and was not removed.</p></li>
</ol>

Because a staged family application can have a document removed by its owner before
submission, a family can already withdraw something they uploaded in error. That is a
different thing from disposal of a record staff have accepted.

## Backups

Backup and restore practices, including how long backups are kept and how a restore honours
a deletion that has since happened, are infrastructure decisions that interact with this
policy. A retention schedule is incomplete until it says what happens to backups.
