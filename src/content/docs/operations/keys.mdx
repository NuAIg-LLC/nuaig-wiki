Encryption is only as good as the handling of the keys. This page describes the kinds of
secret the platform holds and how they are rotated without taking the product down or losing
data.

## The kinds of secret

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Field encryption key</dt><dd>Protects sensitive fields in resident, financial, care, document, qualification and workflow records.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Identity-number vault key</dt><dd>Protects the separate store of identity numbers. Kept distinct from the field key so the blast radius of each is smaller.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Portal token and session keys</dt><dd>Protect family invitations and sessions.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Staff session key</dt><dd>Protects the encrypted server-side session cookie.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Notification data key and destination hash key</dt><dd>Encrypt queued messages, and hash destinations for suppression matching without storing them in the clear.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Audit-chain seed</dt><dd>The starting value of each organisation's hash chain. It is independent of every other secret.</dd></div>
</div>

Different families of stored data also use different **encryption purposes**. Unit pricing
payloads, for example, have a purpose of their own, so a flaw or a leak affecting one family
does not decrypt another.

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Versioned envelopes</dt><dd>Stored ciphertext carries a version and a key identifier. The active key identifier is a setting, and retired keys sit in a decrypt-only set. A retired key cannot also be the active one.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The re-encryption task</dt><dd>A maintenance task finds rows still under an old key and rewrites them under the active one. It covers every encrypted column, including those added by later features.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Authenticated encryption</dt><dd>Because ciphertext is authenticated, a truncated or tampered value is rejected rather than decrypted into garbage. A non-canonical envelope is refused as well.</dd></div>
</div>

## Properties rotation must preserve

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>Purpose</h4>
    <p>Every column keeps its encryption purpose. A rewrite that moved a value to a different purpose would silently change what can decrypt it. The registry of purposes is the source of truth.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>Completeness</h4>
    <p>Any newly introduced encrypted column must be added to the rotation task, or it will be stranded on a key nobody is rotating.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>Readability throughout</h4>
    <p>At no point may a stored value be undecryptable by the keys currently configured.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## What is not covered by the task

Rotating the identity-number vault key is a distinct procedure from rotating the field key,
because that store has a different structure. Treat it as its own change with its own
plan. Custody of keys in dedicated hardware or a managed service is an infrastructure
decision that the platform supports through how secrets are supplied, and does not itself
provide.

## Starting safely

In a production setting the platform **refuses to start** if any of these hold:

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>A development default</dt><dd>Any key or seed that still equals its development value.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>A weak secret</dt><dd>A secret shorter than the minimum.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>A legacy key that is also active</dt><dd>The retired set may not contain the active key.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Development sign-in enabled</dt><dd>The stand-in identity mechanism is on.</dd></div>
</div>

## Operational guidance

<ol class="steps not-content">
  <li><h4>Rotate on a schedule and on suspicion</h4><p>Set a regular cadence, and rotate immediately if exposure is suspected.</p></li>
  <li><h4>Rotate one kind at a time</h4><p>Combining changes makes a failure hard to attribute.</p></li>
  <li><h4>Verify before retiring</h4><p>Confirm no row remains under the old key before removing it from the decrypt-only set.</p></li>
  <li><h4>Keep the previous secrets until verified</h4><p>You may need to roll back.</p></li>
  <li><h4>Record each rotation</h4><p>Who, when, which key, and the verification result.</p></li>
</ol>
