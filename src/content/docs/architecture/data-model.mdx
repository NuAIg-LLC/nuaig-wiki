## The families

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Organisation</dt><dd>The client, its communities, its staff, and each person's membership and role in each community. Break-glass grants and report grants also live here.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The resident</dt><dd>The resident record with its stage and care level, the verified financial profile, care context, any responsible party, and the single separate store for identity numbers.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Evidence</dt><dd>Documents with their kind, extraction and review status, and the staged family application with its sections, documents, invitations and collaborators.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Assessment</dt><dd>Runway assessments and the human decisions that cite them.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Life-plan</dt><dd>Central policy and community pricing as versioned records, completed qualifications as immutable snapshots, observations, advisory analyses and the final decision.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Review</dt><dd>The review policy, and the append-only events that form each qualification's approval chain.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Pricing</dt><dd>Units, their dated price schedules, prior sales and corrections, quotes and the decisions on price reductions, and the approver lists.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Operations</dt><dd>The message queue, delivery events and suppressions, idempotency records, rate-limit counters, portal sessions and events, and the audit ledger.</dd></div>
</div>

## What is immutable

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel panel--ok">
    <h4>Inserted once, never changed</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Qualification snapshots</li>
      <li>Policy and pricing versions</li>
      <li>Review events and final decisions</li>
      <li>Observations and their corrections</li>
      <li>Advisory analyses</li>
      <li>Price schedules, prior-sale revisions, quotes and quote decisions</li>
      <li>Approver list versions</li>
      <li>The audit ledger</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>Mutable, with care</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>The resident's stage and details</li>
      <li>The financial profile, replaced by a verified one</li>
      <li>A unit's inventory record, changed by revision</li>
      <li>Memberships and account state</li>
      <li>Delivery state for a queued message</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

The runtime database identity is granted only insert and read on the immutable tables, so
immutability is a property of the permissions and not just of the application's good
behaviour. A recorded decision also cannot be deleted.

## What is encrypted

Sensitive content is stored as ciphertext in the column, and only queryable metadata stays
in the clear.

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>In the clear</dt><dd>Identifiers, timestamps, relationships, stage, unit codes and dates. Enough to route, order and audit.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Encrypted</dt><dd>Names, dates of birth, financial amounts, care detail, document content, extracted values, review reasons and notes, qualification snapshots, summaries, and the stored responses to repeated requests.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Held apart</dt><dd>Identity numbers are replaced by a token on the resident record and held in a separate store. The full number is never returned to any role.</dd></div>
</div>

Different families of data use **different encryption purposes**, so a key or a leak
affecting one does not become a master key for the rest. See <a href="/operations/keys/">Keys and rotation</a>.

## Every record knows where it belongs

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Organisation</dt><dd>Every record carries its organisation, and the store refuses to show it to anyone else.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Community</dt><dd>Resident records also carry their community, and related records are tied to the same community through composite references. A document cannot be attached to a resident in a different building, and a quote cannot point at another community's unit.</dd></div>
</div>

## Corrections are records

Nothing is silently edited. A corrected observation is a new observation that points at the
earlier one. A corrected prior sale is a new revision of the same sale. A note correction
references the original note. In each case the earlier record stays readable, because the
question "what did we know then" must always have an answer.
