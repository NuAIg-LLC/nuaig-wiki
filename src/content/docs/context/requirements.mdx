These requirements came out of structured discovery with a life-plan operator, and they are
the clearest statement of what the product is for. They are numbered **R1 to R14** and the
numbers are used throughout the organisation, so they are kept stable here.

Each row names the requirement and the part of the product that answers it. The product
supplies the **mechanism**. The **policy numbers** that drive it, such as thresholds and
growth rates, always belong to the client and are entered by them.

## The fourteen requirements

| ID | The client asked for | How the product answers it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R1** | Three financial outcomes, with thresholds set centrally: a definite fit, probably not so consider another unit, and not qualified. | Three classified outcomes plus an explicit **unconfigured** state until thresholds are approved. See <a href="/model/outcomes/">Outcomes and thresholds</a>. |
| **R2** | Live scenarios across unit types, entrance fees and asset strategies. | The household workspace and the <a href="/screens/modeling/">projection lab</a> recalculate on demand without saving anything. |
| **R3** | Per-person and combined finances for couples, including income changes after a death. | Each resident has their own income, assets and care path, with explicit survivor percentages. See <a href="/model/life-plan/">The life-plan model</a>. |
| **R4** | A central contract catalogue assigned to communities, where communities set fees but not actuarial assumptions. | Central policy owns contracts, rates and thresholds. A community supplies only prices for contracts assigned to it. See <a href="/model/configuration/">Configuration and settings</a>. |
| **R5** | Health-based duration adjustments, subject to legal review. | No automatic path from health to outcome. Care durations come from named profiles a reviewer selects. See <a href="/model/ai-boundary/">Where AI starts and stops</a>. |
| **R6** | Unit-level pricing from a schedule, where increases are allowed and decreases need approval. | Dated, source-referenced price schedules. A decrease needs an independent named approver. See <a href="/flows/pricing/">Units, pricing and quotes</a>. |
| **R7** | Prior sale price and contract for a unit, visible to sales. | Prior sales and outstanding refund obligations are kept with the unit and shown when quoting. |
| **R8** | A place to record exceptions and promises made to a family. | Permanent, encrypted notes on the qualification, correctable but never editable. See <a href="/screens/workflow/">Qualification workflow</a>. |
| **R9** | A financial form with instrument types such as pensions, retirement accounts and trusts. | Typed assets with owner and availability month, plus verified document kinds for each type of evidence. |
| **R10** | A printed or emailed prospect summary that needs no prospect login. | A strict-whitelist printable summary staff prepare and share through an approved channel. See <a href="/screens/summary/">Prospect summary</a>. |
| **R11** | Financial and account-bearing documents restricted to sales, the director and escalation contacts. | Financial and health reads are separate scopes. Documents are gated by kind. See <a href="/people/minimum-necessary/">Minimum necessary access</a>. |
| **R12** | Secure central storage of qualifications, with retention subject to legal advice. | Immutable, encrypted snapshots. The product never deletes evidentiary records automatically. See <a href="/operations/retention/">Retention and legal hold</a>. |
| **R13** | Restricted leadership reporting, for example the last ten qualifications in a community. | Cross-community reports behind explicit per-community grants. See <a href="/flows/reporting/">Leadership reporting</a>. |
| **R14** | Historical trends, including residents who outlived their funds. | Dated observations recorded against the original forecast, which is never rewritten. See <a href="/flows/observations/">After move-in</a>. |

## Workflow requirements outside the numbered list

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Flexible order of prerequisites</dt><dd>Four or five review steps may be completed in any order, with the health assessment usually last. The workflow treats each prerequisite as independent and infers no sequence.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Joint decisions and committees</dt><dd>Sales and the director decide together, and some communities add a committee. The chain is configurable per organisation, with distinct named people in each slot.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Short entrance-fee deferral pending a home sale</dt><dd>Modelled as timed net proceeds arriving in a stated month. The entrance fee itself is still taken at month zero, so a deferral is never silently assumed.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Evidence collected from several sources</dt><dd>Tax pages, institution statements, property value less mortgage, and insurance. Each is a document kind a person verifies.</dd></div>
</div>

## Decisions that belong to the client

Several requirements depend on a decision the client has to make and own. The product is
designed so that none of them can be answered by an engineer by accident.

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Outcome thresholds</dt><dd>Where "definite fit" begins and "not qualified" ends. Until approved, the product returns the unconfigured state.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Use of health information</dt><dd>Whether, and how, health may influence a projected stay. Until counsel rules, the product does not use it.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Retention periods and legal holds</dt><dd>How long each class of record is kept. The product invents none.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Authoritative pricing source</dt><dd>Whether prices come from a schedule or a live system. The product records the source of every price it holds.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Named approvers</dt><dd>Who may waive, escalate or approve a price reduction. An empty list grants no authority.</dd></div>
</div>

See <a href="/decisions/open-questions/">Open questions</a> for the full list and its owners.
