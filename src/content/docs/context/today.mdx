Every feature in this product exists because of something a person was already doing by
hand. This page describes that process in the form most life-plan operators run it, so the
rest of the wiki has something to be compared against.

## Where it breaks, and what the product does about each

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Evidence is scattered</dt><dd>Two years of tax pages, investment face sheets, home valuation less mortgage, an insurance policy that may have lapsed. The product collects these through a guided family application and treats each document as something a person must verify. See <a href="/flows/documents/">Documents and verification</a>.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Couples are modelled loosely</dt><dd>Two people have separate income and assets, and one of them leaving changes what the survivor receives. The product models each resident separately, with explicit survivor percentages. See <a href="/model/life-plan/">The life-plan model</a>.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>"Marginal" has no definition</dt><dd>The product makes the thresholds central, versioned and approved by named people, and refuses to classify anything until they exist. See <a href="/model/outcomes/">Outcomes and thresholds</a>.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Approval chains are informal</dt><dd>Who must agree, in what order, who may waive what: all of it lives in people's heads. The product makes the chain configurable and records every step. See <a href="/flows/decision/">Review, sign-off and decision</a>.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Exceptions vanish</dt><dd>A promise made to a family ("we will honour this price") is remembered by whoever made it. The product keeps exceptions as permanent notes tied to the qualification. See <a href="/screens/workflow/">Qualification workflow</a>.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Pricing is manual</dt><dd>Unit prices come from a schedule, and the history of what the same unit sold for before is found by hand. The product keeps dated schedules and prior sales together. See <a href="/flows/pricing/">Units, pricing and quotes</a>.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Access is too wide</dt><dd>Anyone who can open the file sees everything in it. The product limits financial detail to the people who need it. See <a href="/people/minimum-necessary/">Minimum necessary access</a>.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Nobody learns from outcomes</dt><dd>Nothing compares what was forecast to what actually happened. The product records observations against the original forecast. See <a href="/flows/observations/">After move-in</a>.</dd></div>
</div>

## What the manual process does well

It is worth being fair to it. A spreadsheet is fast, flexible and understood by the people
who use it. A sales person can change a number and see the result immediately, in front of
the family. The platform has to keep that, which is why it includes a <a href="/screens/modeling/">projection lab</a>
and a household workspace that recalculates on demand, while moving everything that must be
governed (the rates, the thresholds, the approvals) out of the hands of whoever happens to
hold the file.

## Commercial mechanics around qualification

Qualification sits inside a larger sales process that the platform deliberately does not
own: waiting lists, deposits, unit holds and the time between deposit and closing. Those
are customer-relationship concerns, and organisations differ widely in how they run them.
The platform records the financial qualification and the decision; it does not take
payments or manage a waiting list.
