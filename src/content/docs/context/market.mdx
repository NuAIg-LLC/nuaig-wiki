A product like this has four distinct groups of people around it, and they want different
things. Mixing them up is the most common way to misread a requirement.

## The buyer

A **client organisation** operates several communities and carries the financial liability
of every admission. Its finance leadership owns the definition of every outcome: the
thresholds, the growth rates, the assumed care durations, the contract catalogue. They are
the people who must be able to say "this is our policy" and show who approved it.

What they need from the product:

- One definition of an outcome across all their communities
- Assumptions that cannot be changed without leaving a trace
- Visibility across communities without being handed every resident's file
- A defensible record when a decision is later questioned

## The users

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Admissions counselors</dt><dd>Own intake. They meet families, send invitations, chase documents and review what comes back. They run the conversation but do not see the money.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Sales financial reviewers</dt><dd>Collect and verify financial evidence, run live scenarios with families, produce quotes and complete qualifications. They cannot sign a final decision.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Finance reviewers</dt><dd>Confirm financial profiles, run assessments, validate family-submitted figures and answer exceptions.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Executive directors</dt><dd>The human of record. They see everything relevant to a case and are the only role that can record a decision.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Client administrators</dt><dd>Manage staff, communities, policy, portal branding and access. They configure the product but do not see residents.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Auditors</dt><dd>Read-only oversight, including the full trail. They cannot change anything.</dd></div>
</div>

## The subjects

Families are the people whose finances are modelled, and they are not users in the usual
sense. A family completes an application through a separate, isolated surface using a link
and a one-time code. They never receive a staff account, and they see none of the
qualification machinery. What they can take away is a single printed summary, prepared by
staff.

This matters for design. Anything a family can see must be written in plain language,
because the person reading it is often a worried adult child making a decision about a
parent, not a finance professional.

## The maker

The platform is operated by its maker on behalf of many client organisations at once. The
maker provisions each client, activates it, and can repair a locked-out administrator. The
maker deliberately holds **no access to resident data**: the platform administrator role
carries no resident-data capability at all.

## Why several clients on one platform

Each client differs in small, important ways: how many approvals they require, what they
call a level of care, whether a refund counts towards a threshold. The platform handles
this with settings rather than code, and the rule is strict: **no client-specific
behaviour is ever written into the product.** A difference between two clients is always a
setting whose default preserves existing behaviour. See
<a href="/model/configuration/">Configuration and settings</a>.
