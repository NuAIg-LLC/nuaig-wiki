## The promise a community makes

Many senior-living communities sell a **life-plan contract**. The resident pays a large
entrance fee and then monthly fees, and the community promises care for the rest of their
life, including through higher levels of care that cost far more than the level they move
in at.

That promise is the product. It is also the risk. The contract does not say "until your
money runs out". It says the resident stays.

The shortfall becomes a **benevolence cost**: care provided below its price, funded by
everyone else's monthly fees. A single mistaken admission can be a seven-figure liability
spread across a decade or more. The cost does not appear in the year of the mistake, which
is exactly why it is easy to make.

## So the question is a lifetime question

A monthly-rent question ("can they pay next month's bill?") is easy and nearly useless.
The question that matters is much harder:

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>How long will they live here?</dt><dd>Not a single number. A stay has a length at each level of care, and each of those is uncertain.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>What will it cost them, month by month?</dt><dd>Fees rise each year, and rise differently at each level of care. A couple's costs change when one of them leaves.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>What will they have to pay it with?</dt><dd>Income that may or may not continue to a surviving spouse, savings that earn something while they last, a house that sells in six months, an insurance benefit that pays only for certain care.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>When, if ever, does it fail?</dt><dd>The first month the balance goes negative is the shortfall. Later windfalls do not undo it.</dd></div>
</div>

## Why a spreadsheet cannot carry this

The usual tool for this is a spreadsheet, one copy per community, maintained by whoever
built it. It works until it does not, and the ways it fails are structural.

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>It reads the clock</h4>
    <p>A formula that uses today's date gives a different answer when the file is reopened. The figure that was approved is no longer reproducible. For a record that may be evidence years later, that is disqualifying.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>It has no shared definition</h4>
    <p>Each community decides for itself what counts as a marginal case. Leadership cannot compare two communities, because the word does not mean the same thing in both.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>It runs one projection</h4>
    <p>It returns an ending balance and nothing about how confident anyone should be in it. A household that clears the line by a dollar looks identical to one that clears it by a million.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>It cannot be governed</h4>
    <p>Anyone with the file can change a rate. There is no record of who changed which assumption, when, or whether anyone approved it.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>It cannot protect what it holds</h4>
    <p>A file containing a family's finances, identity and health is copied, emailed and left on laptops. Nothing limits who sees which part of it.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>It leaves no trail</h4>
    <p>If a decision is later questioned, there is no reliable record of what was entered, what was verified, and who agreed.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## What this platform changes

It replaces the spreadsheet with a governed, auditable answer, and it keeps the part of
the spreadsheet people liked, which is that a sales person can sit with a family and see
how the numbers move.
