## What it is for

A family wants to know "what if". What if their income rose, what if they contributed more,
what if the stay were shorter? The lab lets a reviewer answer in the room, immediately,
without creating or altering a record.

## How it behaves

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>A baseline and a scenario</dt><dd>The lab starts from where the resident stands now. Every change is measured against that baseline, and each measure shows its previous value beneath the new one.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Five measures</dt><dd>Runway, Medicaid crossover, funding gap, projected care cost and the risk tier.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Money over time</dt><dd>A chart of countable assets falling towards the Medicaid threshold, so the shape of the problem is visible, not just its end point.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The factors</dt><dd>Sliders for monthly income, liquid assets and family contribution, plus a level of care and the community's price for it.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Reset</dt><dd>Returns every factor to the baseline in one action.</dd></div>
</div>

## What it deliberately is not

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>A second source of truth</h4>
    <p>Nothing here is stored, attached to the resident or visible to anyone else. It disappears when the page does.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>An approximation</h4>
    <p>It calls the same deterministic calculation as a real assessment. The numbers it shows are the numbers a saved assessment would show for the same inputs.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>For anyone</h4>
    <p>It shows money, so it is available only to roles that may see money.</p>
  </div>
</div>

The lab belongs to the runway model. For lifetime contracts, the equivalent is comparing a
baseline with an alternative inside the <a href="/screens/life-plan/">life-plan workspace</a>.
