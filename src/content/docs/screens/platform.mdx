## The banner is the point

The control plane is the one place where a person acts outside any single client's
boundary. The product refuses to let that feel routine. A persistent banner says so on every
page, and it also says that every change is recorded in the **target organisation's own
audit trail**, so a client can see what the maker did to it.

## Provisioning an organisation

A single request creates three things: the organisation, its first community, and its first
administrator. The organisation starts in **provisioning** and stays there until every
activation precondition is met.

## What the platform administrator can do

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Create</dt><dd>Provision a new organisation.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Activate and suspend</dt><dd>Move an organisation through its lifecycle. Suspension, with a recorded reason, refuses every one of its staff at sign-in.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Edit identity settings</dt><dd>The identity issuer and related settings. A typo at provisioning is not permanent.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Repair an administrator</dt><dd>Recover an organisation that has locked itself out, by activating or re-binding its administrator.</dd></div>
</div>

## What it can never do

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>Read a resident</h4>
    <p>The platform role carries no resident-data capability. It can run an organisation's lifecycle without ever becoming a member of its clinical or financial workforce.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>List organisations by reaching into their data</h4>
    <p>The directory reads through a single hardened lookup that returns control-plane metadata and readiness figures only. It is not a bypass of the isolation, and the repair writes enter the target organisation's own boundary.</p>
  </div>
</div>
