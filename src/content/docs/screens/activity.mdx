## What an entry says

Each row records **when**, **who**, **what happened**, **what it touched**, **which fields
were touched** and **where it originated**. Reading a sensitive record is an event just as
writing one is, and the fields named are the classified ones, so the log can answer an
accounting-of-disclosures question.

## Filters

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Person</dt><dd>Anyone, or one named person. The list is built from the people who appear on the page when the viewer cannot manage staff.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Action</dt><dd>Any action, or one kind such as create, update, read or decide.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Resource type</dt><dd>An exact, lowercase type, for example a policy record.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Time range</dt><dd>Any time, or a window.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Narrow this page</dt><dd>A text filter over the rows already loaded.</dd></div>
</div>

## It says what it excludes

Rather than silently dropping rows a viewer may not see, the page tells them. An auditor
who also holds resident access sees a banner saying the feed includes resident-linked
events for the facility they have selected. Someone who holds audit access without resident
access sees the administrative half and is told so.
