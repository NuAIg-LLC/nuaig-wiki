  - "Families never sign in this way. They use an invitation link and a one-time code."

The product deliberately does not run its own passwords. Each client organisation connects
its existing corporate identity system, so staff sign in with the credentials they already
have, with whatever multi-factor policy that organisation enforces, and are removed the
moment the organisation removes them.

## What the sign-in gives a person

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>An identity</dt><dd>Who they are, according to their organisation. Carried as coordinates only: the product never takes privilege from the token.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>A working community</dt><dd>If they belong to more than one building, they choose which one they are working in from the top bar. The choice is cleared whenever the identity changes, so one person's selection can never leak into the next sign-in.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>A session</dt><dd>Held on the server in an encrypted, HTTP-only cookie, refreshed quietly near expiry. The access token is never exposed to the page.</dd></div>
</div>

## What the product refuses

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>Account not ready</h4>
    <p>A person whose organisation has not finished onboarding, whose account is inactive, or who has no identity bound yet cannot sign in.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>Suspended organisation</h4>
    <p>When the maker suspends an organisation, every one of its staff is refused at sign-in, immediately.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel panel--stop">
    <h4>No quiet switching</h4>
    <p>There is no way for a visitor to pick an identity. The development stand-in that exists for building the product is unreachable in production by three independent checks.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Why a person lands where they do

Everyone starts on their home screen, and the left navigation shows only the areas their
role may open. A counselor sees the overview, pipeline, family portal and review queue. A
client administrator sees the administration console. A platform administrator sees only the
platform control plane. The navigation is generated from the same rules the records
service enforces, so it can never offer a door that is locked.
