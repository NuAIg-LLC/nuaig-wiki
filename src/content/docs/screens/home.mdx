## What it is for

It is the screen a person opens first thing and the one that tells them what to do next.
Nothing on it is a report. Every number is a doorway into the work it counts.

## What is on it

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Workload counters</dt><dd>Open screenings, residents ready for a decision, documents to review, and cases open for fourteen days or more. Each counter links into the pipeline already filtered to those residents.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>The resident worklist</dt><dd>Residents grouped by the step they are genuinely waiting on, such as "Assessed, awaiting a decision". Each card shows the care level, the current step and the days since intake. A search finds a resident by name, care level or state, and includes decided residents.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Assessment coverage</dt><dd>How many residents have an assessment on file, and how many do not yet.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Risk and funding runway</dt><dd>The distribution of risk tiers and of runway across the facility's latest assessments. Dollar aggregates appear only for roles with the financial scope.</dd></div>
</div>

## Behaviour worth knowing

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>It respects the role</h4>
    <p>Counts and lists cover only what the person may see. Someone without the financial scope sees the distributions but no dollar aggregates.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>It is scoped to one building</h4>
    <p>Everything counts the community selected in the top bar. Switching community recalculates it. A person never sees a total across buildings they have not been given.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>It refreshes on request</h4>
    <p>A Refresh control reloads the figures. Nothing updates under the person's hands while they work.</p>
  </div>
</div>
