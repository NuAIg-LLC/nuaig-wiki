## The six step states

The rail does not just list steps. It tells each viewer how they stand with each one.

<div class="defs not-content">
  <div><dt>Done</dt><dd>Complete. The step stays reachable so its content can be reviewed.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Started</dt><dd>Some work is recorded but the step is not finished.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Current</dt><dd>The step this viewer should act on next.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Blocked</dt><dd>Waiting on something earlier. The rail names what.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Not mine</dt><dd>Belongs to a role this viewer does not hold. The rail names the owner and the reason, so a counselor sees "handled by finance" instead of a bare refusal.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Upcoming</dt><dd>Not reached yet.</dd></div>
</div>

## Behaviour worth knowing

<div class="split not-content">
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>It heads to the right place</h4>
    <p>When a record opens, it lands on the step this viewer should act on, not always on the first one.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>It never offers a refused action</h4>
    <p>The step's heading and button reflect what the viewer's role can actually do. A finance step does not present a counselor with a button that would fail.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h4>It explains a blocked step</h4>
    <p>The panel gives a short "who does this next" card and a way to skip ahead to the next step the viewer can act on.</p>
  </div>
</div>
